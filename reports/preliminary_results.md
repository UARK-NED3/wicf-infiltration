# Preliminary screening results

Generated by `py -3 scripts/run_screening.py` from repository root on local execution.

## Evidence and boundary

These are **simulated/derived screening results**. The baseline assumes a 0.90 m × 2.10 m personnel door opening from a 24 C, 50% RH inventory room; no actual facility geometry, traffic, HVAC pressure, curtain state, or refrigeration response is represented. The model uses an analytical two-layer hydrostatic exchange approximation, `C_d = 0.60`, effective-exchange multiplier = 0.40, and a 3 s linear flow-establishment ramp. ASHRAE reports that established cold-room doorway flow occurs about 3 s after opening; the multiplier is an unvalidated sensitivity parameter, not a measured curtain credit.

## Modeling system

**System boundary.** One open, vertical personnel doorway connects an adjacent inventory-room air volume (state `o`) to a well-mixed WICF air volume (state `i`). The calculated output is the *thermal load carried by infiltrating air during one door-open event*, referenced to the indoor WICF air state. It includes the sensible and water-vapor enthalpy difference of the incoming air. It excludes panel conduction, products, people, lighting, fans, defrost, refrigerant-side dynamics, and any downstream compressor electrical-energy calculation.

**Coordinates and sign convention.** `z = 0` is the door sill and `z = H/2` is the assumed neutral plane. The colder, denser WICF air leaves through the lower half of the doorway; the same mass rate of warmer air enters through the upper half. Positive `Q_event` is an added refrigeration load. Temperatures are in degrees C, pressures in Pa, mass flow in kg/s, and energy in kJ/event.

## Governing equations

The code evaluates moist-air state properties at 101325 Pa. The saturation vapor pressure `p_ws(T)` is calculated using a Buck-type approximation (separate water/ice coefficients above/below 0 C). For each air state:

```text
p_v = phi p_ws(T)
w = 0.621945 p_v / (p - p_v)
h = 1.006 T + w (2501.0 + 1.86 T)                         [kJ/kg dry air]
rho = (p - p_v) / [R_da (T + 273.15)] + p_v / [R_v (T + 273.15)]  [kg/m3]
```

Here `phi` is relative humidity as a fraction; `w` is humidity ratio (kg water/kg dry air); `h` is moist-air enthalpy; `rho` is moist-air density; `R_da = 287.055 J/(kg K)` and `R_v = 461.495 J/(kg K)`.

For `rho_i > rho_o`, the established cold-air outflow is an analytical hydrostatic integration over the lower half-door. Its magnitude is also taken as the incoming warm-air mass-flow magnitude:

```text
m_dot_ex = M C_d W rho_i sqrt[2 g (rho_i - rho_o) / rho_i] (2/3) (H/2)^(3/2)   [kg/s]
```

where `W` and `H` are doorway width and height, `g = 9.80665 m/s2`, `C_d` is discharge coefficient, and `M` is the effective-exchange multiplier. A linear establishment ramp is imposed to represent the initial transient:

```text
F(t) = t/(2 t_e),                         0 < t < t_e
F(t) = 1 - t_e/(2 t),                     t >= t_e
Q_event(t) = m_dot_ex t F(t) [1/(1+w_o)] max(0, h_o - h_i)   [kJ]
```

`t_e` is establishment time (3 s baseline). The factor `1/(1+w_o)` converts moist-air mass to dry-air mass before applying the dry-air-basis enthalpy difference.

## Assumptions and validity limits

| Assumption | Baseline value / treatment | Consequence if false |
|---|---|---|
| Door and states | 0.90 m × 2.10 m; 24 C, 50% RH room; 2 C cooler or -18 C freezer | Actual geometry and adjacent-room psychrometrics directly change the load. |
| Exchange physics | Two-way buoyancy exchange, neutral plane at `H/2`; no imposed pressure difference | HVAC imbalance, wind, pallet/forklift wake, or traffic can dominate the predicted flow. |
| WICF state | Uniform and fixed during an event | Does not predict room-temperature excursion, product temperature, frost, or recovery dynamics. |
| Flow coefficients | `C_d = 0.60`; `M = 0.40` | These are assumed screening parameters, not calibrated curtain or obstruction credits. |
| Initial transient | Linear 0-to-established flow over `t_e = 3 s` | Short-open-event loads may be biased if the actual flow develops differently. |
| Psychrometrics | 101325 Pa; equilibrium humidity property approximations | Elevation and sensor bias have small-to-moderate effects relative to unmeasured traffic/pressure in this application. |

## Baseline results

| Door-open duration (s) | 2 C cooler load (kJ/event) | -18 C freezer load (kJ/event) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 15 | 121 | 313 |
| 30 | 256 | 661 |
| 45 | 390 | 1009 |
| 60 | 524 | 1357 |

The corresponding modelled established exchange flows are 0.244 kg/s (cooler) and 0.362 kg/s (freezer). Loads scale approximately linearly after the 3 s establishment period within this fixed-state model. The freezer has the larger per-event thermal burden because its indoor-air enthalpy is lower and density contrast is larger.

## Climate sensitivity (not actual room conditions)

The public input is the exact [Open-Meteo Archive API request used in this analysis](https://archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive?latitude=36.3729&longitude=-94.2088&start_date=2025-01-01&end_date=2025-12-31&daily=temperature_2m_mean,relative_humidity_2m_mean&timezone=America%2FChicago): 2025 daily 2 m mean temperature and relative humidity at a Bentonville, AR proxy coordinate. Its cached copy is `data/raw/bentonville_ar_2025_daily_weather.json`; the [Open-Meteo historical-data documentation](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api) describes the API and reanalysis sources. The 2025 daily reanalysis input yields 60 s door-open loads spanning 0–1013 kJ for the cooler and 14–2030 kJ for the freezer. This sensitivity supports logging the *adjacent-room* temperature/RH and pressure rather than relying on outdoor weather.

## Preliminary conclusion

An escalating workflow is technically more defensible than one immediate nuisance-prone alarm. For this baseline, evaluate an attention/close-door alert at **15–30 s** and a high-priority alarm at **45–60 s**. The hypothesis is that these thresholds capture the onset of repeated quasi-steady exchange while preserving normal short personnel passages. The thresholds are **not validated settings** and must not be deployed based on this analysis alone.

## Required validation before an operational recommendation

1. Measure actual open duration and traffic distribution by door and shift.
2. Measure adjacent-room/WICF T-RH and differential pressure at 1 Hz or faster; record door state and curtain configuration.
3. Estimate event load from synchronized refrigeration power, suction/evaporator measurements, or a calibrated thermal balance; quantify sensor uncertainty and event-to-event variability.
4. Compare no curtain, strip curtain, and operationally acceptable closure scenarios. Do not assume an air/strip curtain reduces load without measurement.
5. Select thresholds against false-alarm burden, safety/egress constraints, product-temperature excursions, and energy/refrigeration response—not an energy-only model.
